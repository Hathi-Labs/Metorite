## Description

Result of auditing our implementation against `meowcaller`'s source, after calls connect but carry no audible audio.

## What the audit cleared

**Attach order is correct.** `Receive` is attached immediately after `Call()` returns, before the peer answers — the same order the library's own `examples/cli` uses.

**Not subscribing a Player is correct**, and was my leading suspicion until the source ruled it out. From `engine_media.go`:

> Send loop: frame-paced from connect, **NOT gated on the Player**. WhatsApp starts media on relay connection and the relay learns our SSRC from our FIRST RTP — it won't bridge the peer's media until it sees our stream. So we send silence frames until the Player has real audio.

So outbound silence flows without `Subscribe`, and the relay bridge forms. That was worth verifying rather than assuming, since "you must send to receive" would have been a real bug.

## What was actually wrong

We made the library mute. `resolveConfig` defaults to `zerolog.Nop()`, so without `WithLogger` **every media diagnostic is discarded** — including the four lines that diagnose a call you can't hear:

- `first RTP sent to relay, outbound media flowing`
- `relay silent after allocate, no bytes back yet (allocate undelivered or rejected)`
- `first authenticated peer SRTCP received`
- `peer SRTCP failed authentication`

That's why the bridge log has had nothing to say about media. The library's own example passes `WithLogger(*zerolog.Ctx(ctx))`; we passed nothing.

## Change

`WithLogger` wired to stdout (where journalctl captures it), tagged `component=meowcaller` and per-account so one call is greppable out of a busy bridge, with `WHATSAPP_BRIDGE_CALL_LOG_LEVEL` for the full per-packet trace.

## Verification

Go builds, vets and tests clean; `zerolog` promoted to a direct dependency.

## What this does and doesn't do

It does not make audio audible. It makes the reason visible — which, for a connected call with no media, is the necessary next step and currently impossible.
