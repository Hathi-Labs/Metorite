## Description

Counting agreements measures **consistency, not correctness**. A classifier confidently wrong about one sender is wrong the same way every time — and a streak of three was the *entire* bar for writing a permanent sender pin.

## We had copied half of upstream

Inbox Zero's [`analyze-sender-pattern`](reference/inbox-zero/apps/web/app/api/ai/analyze-sender-pattern/route.ts) uses its threshold as a **floor**, then runs a dedicated second LLM call over up to 10 of the sender's messages asking a *different question* — not "what is this email?" but **"will every future email from this address serve the same purpose?"**, at 90%+ confidence or nothing.

We had the floor and none of the judgment.

## Three changes

**Ported the second opinion.** `_ai_confirms_sender_pattern` judges the sender's 10 most recent messages, with upstream's guidance made explicit: no people, no generic domains unless plainly automated, no mixed content, any doubt is a no. **Fails closed at every step** — no model, too few samples, unparseable answer, exception → no pattern. Not learning is free; a wrong pin is silent, permanent, and short-circuits the classifier that would have caught it.

**Floor raised 3 → 5.** Three is short enough to reach by accident. No longer the only bar, but still a real one.

**Scope restricted to Newsletter / Marketing / Cold Email.** A FROM pattern asserts something about the sender's *identity* — that this address only ever sends one kind of thing. True of a list, a blast, an outreach account: the mail is defined by who sent it.

Not true of Receipt, Calendar or Notification, which describe what a message *is* and routinely arrive from people you also converse with. A colleague sends an invite Monday and a question Tuesday; pinned to Calendar, the question is filed as a calendar item. **Two of the patterns purged from the live account were exactly this** (`midhun.vm` → Calendar, `debesh@metafora.sg` → Receipt) and the correspondent check alone hadn't stopped them.

You can still pin any category by hand. This restricts only what the assistant may decide **unattended**.

Ordering is deliberate: cheap local checks gate the one that costs a model call, so the verdict is only asked about a sender that earned it.
