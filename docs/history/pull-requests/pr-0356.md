## Description

## N8 — delete a member permanently, everyone except yourself

**WS-24 / N8** · owning spec `ai-company-brain/specs/colleague_onboarding.md` §2 Step 5.

Asked for: *"allow the owner to be able to remove and completely delete everyone else apart from themselves."*

`DELETE /admin/members/{email}` stays exactly as it was — a soft remove (`status='removed'`, roles dropped, row kept). This adds a **second, harder action beside it**: `DELETE /admin/members/{email}/purge`. `remove_member`'s diff has **zero removed lines**.

### What a purge destroys, and what it keeps

**Deleted** — the identity and everything that exists only to grant them access: role grants, permission overrides, group membership, room participation, app grants, remembered "always allow this tool" decisions, all three credential-bearing accounts (email, WhatsApp, tasks), their **private** chat sessions, their `access_request` row, and finally `app_user`.

**Kept** — the audit trail, their apps, workflows, meetings, notes, **shared** rooms, locally-created tasks and projects, and their CRM `person` record.

The audit trail is not negotiable. `app_audit` already says so in its own schema — `app_id UUID` with no FK, commented *"audit survives hard delete"*. An audit trail that disappears with the person is not one.

**Nothing is anonymised, deliberately.** The address is the join key across ~50 tables, so blanking `owner_email` would not hide the person — it would orphan their apps. "Delete the identity" means removing the `app_user` row and every credential and grant keyed to it, not erasing the address everywhere.

### 🔴 The blast radius is much larger than "one person"

Deleting a credential means deleting the row it lives on, and those rows cascade:

```
email_accounts  ->  17 direct, 20 transitive   the entire mirrored mailbox
wa_accounts     ->  14 direct, 16 transitive   the entire WhatsApp history
task_accounts   ->  gtd_projects, gtd_items (SYNCED only), gtd_waiting
```

Their actual mailbox at Microsoft is untouched; what goes is our mirror plus every rule, label, learned pattern, embedding and contact derived from it. **The confirmation dialog says this before you can confirm**, and the response reports it table by table.

### 🔴 The first version reported destroyed rows as *kept*

Verification caught this and it is the reason for a repair round.

`gtd_items` was on the **keep** list under `lower(user_id) = :email`. `task_accounts` was on the **delete** list. And `48_task_manager_gtd.sql:93` declares `account_id ... REFERENCES task_accounts(id) ON DELETE CASCADE`, while the sync path stamps **both** `user_id` and `account_id` on every synced row.

So a member with 847 synced tasks got `kept: {"tasks": 847}` **while all 847 were destroyed**. `gtd_projects` was destroyed and appeared on neither list. The dialog said "tasks... they authored" under **Kept**.

That is worse than failing to report a destruction — it reports it as a survival, on the one action that cannot be undone.

Fixed: the keep clauses now carry `AND account_id IS NULL`, and synced items and projects are counted and reported on the **deleted** side as `synced_tasks` / `synced_projects`. The dialog names them.

**Why nothing caught it:** the existing fence compared `count_sql` to `delete_sql` *within one row-spec*, and the test fake models no foreign keys at all — so neither a structural nor a behavioural test could see a DELETE on one table silently emptying a table on the KEEP list. There are now two cross-table fences driven by a cascade graph derived from `infra/postgres/*.sql`, plus the fake's docstring stating it models no cascades and why that makes the claim structural.

### Two more gates that were unfenced

**The permission on the most destructive route in the product.** Deleting `require_permission("admin:members:manage")` — leaving the package floor `Depends(require_admin_user)` — left **162 tests green**. The floor is `admin:members:read`, which a seeded `manager` holds, so that mutation silently hands hard-delete of any member to every manager. Now pinned by reading the slug out of the dependency.

**The type-to-confirm gate.** `const confirmed = true;` left 32 pytest and 173 vitest green. The rule is now its own module with its own tests — including that an empty address confirms nothing, which a bare `typed === member.email` would get wrong.

### Self-protection

The purge goes through `_common.assert_not_self_lockout` — invariant 4's shared home — and `assert_owner_survives`. The door-enumeration test now covers **four** doors. Deleting the self-guard fails **8** tests across two files; deleting `assert_owner_survives` fails exactly **1**. The failure sets are disjoint, which is only possible because every case discriminates on detail text (*"cannot permanently delete yourself"* vs *"no owner"*) plus what was and was not written. **No bare status-code assertion anywhere in the file** — both guards answer 409, and that trap has now shipped seven times here.

### 🔴 One decision still open for the owner

`task_accounts` holds an encrypted credential **and** cascades into synced tasks. So a purge deletes a person's ClickUp-synced tasks while keeping their local ones — an asymmetry that falls out of where a foreign key sits, not from anything anyone chose.

- **As built:** delete the row. The credential is genuinely gone, synced tasks go with it, and the confirmation and counts say so.
- **The alternative:** null the credential and keep the row, so the task mirror survives and a husk account row stays behind.

Recorded in the spec as an open question. The reporting is now truthful under either choice.
