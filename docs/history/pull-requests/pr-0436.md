## 1. `WS-27bt` — repaint RapidTool

The complaint looked like a component problem. It was twelve numbers.

`contrast.test.ts` already measured every ink pair and carried a baseline of
**seven known failures**, all in RapidTool, deferred with this note:

> *"fixing them means changing how the shipped product looks — a design decision
> for whoever owns the brand, not a side effect of adding a test."*

The owner has now made that decision, so they are fixed rather than excused.
`KNOWN_SHORTFALLS` is **empty for the first time**:

| pair | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| `warningForeground` on `warning` | **1.50** | 5.55 |
| `successForeground` on `success` | 1.90 | 6.10 |
| `warning` on `card` | 1.57 | 5.55 |
| `success` on `card` | 1.99 | 6.10 |
| `accentForeground` on `accent` | 2.16 | 4.66 |
| `destructiveForeground` on `destructive` (light) | 3.59 | 6.47 |
| `destructiveForeground` on `destructive` (dark) | 3.63 | 5.12 |

1.50:1 is white text on bright yellow — not "slightly low", invisible, and it
shipped for as long as the product has existed.

### What actually made it look flat, which no test could see

`--background` and `--card` were **both `hsl(0 0% 100%)`**. A measured **1.00:1**.
A dashboard of seven cards was seven white rectangles on white, held apart by a
border at 1.23:1. Every ink pair on that screen passed. Two different questions —
"is this readable" and "is this a card" — and only the first had a fence.

### Two new fences (R7)

- **`mutedForeground` on `muted`** — the most common small-text pairing in the
  product, and the failing one at 4.33:1, while the same ink on a white card
  passed at 4.75 and made the token look fine. Measuring an ink against only its
  best background is how a palette ships unreadable in its most-used position.
- **"draws every card as a distinguishable surface"** — `max(elevation, border) ≥ 1.2`
  across all four themes × both modes. EITHER/OR because Material separates by
  tone and Fluent by a line; demanding both would force one idiom on a theming
  engine built to avoid that. ⚠️ Labelled a **design** floor, not WCAG — do not
  cite it as conformance.

### Judgement calls to challenge

**`warning` moved 47° → 32°.** Yellow cannot reach AA on white and still look
yellow; at the required lightness 47° reads olive-brown. Real brand change.
Sidebar tokens deliberately untouched — not in the failing set, and I would have
been guessing at the rail.

Tokens asserted **live in a browser** (fresh context per pair — a leaked init
script is how four "light" screenshots once came out dark and the run still
exited 0). `--cat-*` ramp re-measured against the new page: all 8 still clear AA.

## 2. `WS-27bu` — workload rows show a name

Every row read "kiruba", "jasim", "dev". Not a data problem: `/ops/workload`
already joined `app_user` for capacity and never selected `display_name`.

That join also filtered `WHERE weekly_capacity_hours IS NOT NULL` — so the naive
fix would have worked for whoever had capacity set and silently not for anyone
else. Filter removed; null hours were already handled downstream.

One `personLabel()` helper, not the same ternary at two call sites.

## 3. `WS-27ak-2` — Tooltip

Spec §9.4.2 item 2. Native `title` cannot be themed and **never opens on keyboard
focus**.

### 🔴 Finding: Base UI wires no ARIA association

Verified in a browser. With the tooltip open the trigger carries `id`,
`data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger`, `data-popup-open` — and **no `aria-describedby`**.
`Tooltip.Popup` renders a bare div with **no role and no id** to point at.

The wrapper sets `role="tooltip"` itself and that line is load-bearing — I found
the gap by removing it, believing I was letting the substrate own the ARIA, and
the test went 5/6 → 3/6.

**Consequence stated in the file:** an icon-only trigger MUST carry its own
`aria-label`. `describeOnly` is documented as **inert** rather than left looking
functional; wiring it properly is a separate slice.

Also re-derived from the package rather than recalled: **`delay` is not on
`Tooltip.Root`** — it is `Provider`'s, and nothing mounted one.
