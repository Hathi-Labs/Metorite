## Description

The planner used to fill the whole day wall-to-wall with focus work "as if I am not a human being." Now it plans like a person — and **how** it plans is configurable at two levels: a standing philosophy you set once, and an optional per-run instruction for today.

## Two levels of "how to organize my day"
- **Standing planning philosophy** (Settings → "How should the AI plan your day?"): a free-text instruction the LLM planner obeys on **every** run. Blank falls back to a humane default ("leave breathing room — don't cram every minute; front-load deep work; batch calls; protect time to think").
- **Per-run instruction** (Plan-my-day input, broadened from "How's your energy?"): today's mood/constraints/theme — *"calls only"*, *"deep work"*, *"low energy"*, *"free after 3pm"*, *"commitments at 2pm"*. Flows to the chat planner tools too (`gtd_plan_day(energy_note=…)`).

## A planner that leaves room to be human
- **Breaks** — after `max_focus_run_mins` of continuous focus (default 90), the packer works in a `break_mins` break (default 10). `0` disables it.
- **Protected lunch** — an optional midday window the planner won't book over (off by default; toggle + hours in Settings). Also honored by rollover, replan, and the nightly job.
- **Whitespace** — the philosophy prompt tells the LLM not to fill every minute; combined with breaks/lunch the day breathes. Lunch/break decisions surface in the plan notes.

## Changes
- **Backend:** migration `93_gtd_planning_prefs.sql` (planning_prompt, max_focus_run_mins, break_mins, lunch_start/end_hour on gtd_settings); `settings.py` model/patch/load; `calendar.py` — `DEFAULT_PLANNING_PROMPT`, `_planning_prefs`/`_lunch_interval`, break insertion + lunch protection in `_compute_day_plan` and lunch in the other three planner paths; philosophy fed into the LLM ranking prompt.
- **Frontend:** Calendar-settings popover gains the philosophy textarea + break/lunch controls; `api.ts` + store defaults extended; the Plan-my-day per-run input broadened; chat persona notes the standing philosophy is applied server-side.
